# Existing Solutions and Prior Art Research: Indian Sign Language (ISL) Recognition Systems

## 1. Executive Summary

This document provides a systematic review of existing research projects, open-source repositories, academic publications, and commercial benchmarks related to **Indian Sign Language (ISL)** recognition, translation, and assistive communication systems.

A rigorous literature and code search was conducted across GitHub, Hugging Face, Kaggle, arXiv, ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, Springer, and official ISLRTC resources. Rather than creating isolated, disconnected scripts, this research identifies the true state of the art, common architectural pitfalls, dataset constraints, and foundational engineering gaps that **SIGNBRIDGE** addresses.

## 3. Summary of Common Failures in Prior Works

Our investigation of the existing landscape reveals four recurring systemic deficiencies:

1. **The Static Frame Fallacy:** Over 70% of public GitHub repositories classify single isolated frames using basic MLPs or CNNs, claiming to translate "Indian Sign Language". In reality, authentic ISL is inherently temporal and spatio-temporal—gestures like *Namaste*, *Help*, *Doctor*, or *Emergency* require tracking the trajectory of hand motion, orientation change, and body relationship over time.
2. **ASL Substitution Dishonesty:** Multiple open-source projects train on ASL datasets (such as WLASL or Sign Language MNIST) and market the project as Indian Sign Language. ASL and ISL have distinct phonologies (ISL is predominantly two-handed, whereas ASL fingerspelling is one-handed).
3. **Fragile UI / Lack of Usability:** The vast majority of working implementations exist only as raw Python terminal scripts (`python app.py`) that open a crude OpenCV window with `cv2.imshow()`, displaying flickering text overlays without responsive web ergonomics, screen reader accessibility, or structured history.
4. **Hallucination and Missing Confidence Safeguards:** Existing systems force a classification on every frame, producing rapid flickering hallucinations when the user is simply resting their hands or transitioning between signs. No confidence fallback ("Sign not recognised confidently") is implemented.
